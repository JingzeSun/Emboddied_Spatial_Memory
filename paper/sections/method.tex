\subsection{Memory and the five operations}
At frame $t$ the method receives public observations $O_t$ from the front end and its previous memory $M_{t-1}$, and outputs a frame program $\Pi_t$, a set of operations committed together: $M_t=\mathrm{exec}(M_{t-1},\Pi_t)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}).
No object identity or class, change log or future frame is ever an input; only the choice of $\Pi_t$ is learned.
Each entity record holds an anonymous identity, a state, a version chain, appearance descriptors, the centroid and box of its latest observed surface, its evidence (the fragments it absorbed) and counts, including \emph{missed opportunities} (frames in which it should have been visible but was not matched).
The \emph{map} is the set of active and dormant entities: a \emph{dormant} entity has not been seen for a while but there is no evidence that it is gone, whereas a \emph{retracted} entity has such evidence, leaves the map, and is kept with its closed version so that it can still be recalled and reactivated.
\op{NOOP} keeps an unmatched entity that should be visible and counts a missed opportunity; \op{BIND} attaches a fragment to an active entity; \op{BIRTH} creates an entity from an unassigned fragment; \op{RETRACT} closes the current version of an unmatched entity that should be visible; \op{REACTIVATE} opens a new version of a dormant or retracted entity at the fragment's place, with the same identity.
Two rules are shared: in every arm but \arm{AssocOnly} an active entity becomes dormant after three consecutive missed opportunities, and every 10 frames two active or dormant entities with descriptor cosine $\geq 0.6$, centroids at most 0.5\,m apart and box IoU $\geq 0.05$ are merged into one record that keeps one identity and all evidence.
The executor applies the program to a copy of $M_{t-1}$ and drops it as a whole if any precondition fails (the shared rules still run); history is never deleted.

\subsection{Frozen front end}
\label{sec:frontend}
The \emph{front end}, the perception stage shared by every arm, is frozen.
Fragments come either from the simulator's instance segmentation, of which only anonymous binary masks are exposed (no identities, classes or poses), or from SAM~2.1, a general-purpose learned image segmenter run prompt-free on the whole image (Hiera Small)~\cite{sam2_2025,sam21release}; each covers at least 196 pixels, at most 64 per frame.
A fragment carries its back-projected 3D points, centroid and box, and an appearance descriptor: the mean DINOv2 ViT-B/14 patch feature in its mask (a frozen self-supervised image network~\cite{dinov2_2024}) through a fixed 128-dimensional projection trained once per mask source, with an instance-contrastive loss, on 30 development houses never used for training, selection or test.
Visibility is tested on an entity's last seen surface (up to 64 sampled points per fragment of its latest observation): projected into the current depth image, a point is \emph{seen through} if the measured depth lies more than 5\,cm beyond it, \emph{on the surface} if within 5\,cm, and occluded if nearer.
An entity \emph{should be visible} if at least one point in 64 is seen through or on the surface, and its \emph{free-space coverage} is the seen-through share: the old surface of a removed cup is seen through, while a hidden cup is occluded.
Instance masks remove segmentation errors unrelated to memory; SAM~2.1 tests a learned segmenter.

\subsection{Recall and frame-level joint assignment}
For each fragment $f$, recall unites a local channel (the $k{=}5$ entities with the highest descriptor cosine within 3\,m) and a global channel ($k'{=}3$, any distance, any state including retracted); because recall ignores the entity state, \arm{AssocOnly} receives the same far-away candidates as VSMT-lean.
The cost matrix has a row per fragment and a column per recalled entity plus one \op{BIRTH} column per fragment,
\begin{equation}
C[f,e]=-a(f,e),\qquad C[f,\mathrm{birth}_f]=-b(f),
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
with association and birth logits $a$ and $b$ and a forbidding cost for pairs not recalled.
A minimum-cost rectangular assignment (Hungarian method~\cite{kuhn1955}) is compiled into \op{BIND} (active entity), \op{REACTIVATE} (dormant or retracted entity) or \op{BIRTH}: one solve decides who is whom, who is new and who is back, and the heads only supply the costs.
For every unassigned map entity that should be visible, the existence head then decides \op{RETRACT} or \op{NOOP}.

\subsection{Learned cost heads and training}
The association head $a(f,e)$ reads 14 features of a fragment--entity pair (appearance similarities, distance, box overlap, size ratio, recency, the entity's state and missed opportunities, and the pair's rank among the fragment's candidates); the existence head $r(e)$ reads 17 of an entity (visibility and see-through share, viewing geometry, counts, recency, its best match among current fragments, see-through history); the birth head $b(f)$ reads 4 (best cosine to any entity, active entities within 1\,m, pixel count, valid-depth ratio).
Each head is a two-layer, 128-wide MLP on standardised inputs: 54,787 parameters in all (\arm{AssocOnly}: 35,842).
The loss is a softmax cross-entropy over the recalled and birth columns of each labelled fragment plus a binary cross-entropy for existence with absent rows weighted by $w$, the present-to-absent ratio in the training data; an entity is retracted when its corrected score $\sigma(r(e)-\ln w)$, an estimate of how likely it is no longer where the memory keeps it, reaches a threshold $\tau_r$.
Training uses AdamW for 20 epochs (cosine-decayed learning rate) and five seeds.
Because the memory a head sees depends on its own decisions, training runs in two rounds as in DAgger~\cite{dagger2011}: round~0 learns from memory trajectories of a registered configuration of the rule-based arm \arm{ELU-P}, since no heads exist yet; round~1 adds the trajectories of the round-0 heads, and all results use round~1.
Checkpoints are scored on held-out training houses, never on validation.

\subsection{Hindsight teacher and the candidate-before-teacher seal}
\label{sec:teacher}
The \emph{teacher} turns the simulator's instance ground truth at frame $t$ into labels after the decision; it never uses future frames.
A fragment's dominant object is read from the instance map; an entity \emph{carries} an object when that object is the strict majority of its evidence (otherwise the entity is ambiguous).
The association target is the entity carrying the fragment's object, or \op{BIRTH} if none exists; the existence label is ``absent'' when the entity's object has left the scene or the entity is no longer at it by the node rule (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}).
Recall sets and association and birth features are sealed (digest A) before any ground-truth file is opened, assignments and existence features after the solve (digest B), and ground truth opens only against a record of both digests: the teacher can label sealed rows and columns but never create, remove or reorder candidates.
The seal keeps labels from shaping what the method sees, so the training data contain only what the method could compute itself, and a candidate it failed to recall stays a counted miss.
Every decision thus falls into exactly one class of a \emph{ledger}: \emph{recall miss} (the correct entity was not a candidate, so no choice could be right), \emph{teacher error} (the label itself is ambiguous), \emph{amortization error} (a clear label and the right candidate, but the heads chose otherwise), correct, unlabelled, or duplicate (a second fragment of an object already labelled in the frame, which no method can satisfy since an entity takes one fragment per frame).
The ledger shows where errors arise; it does not by itself make a method better.

The arms differ only in how costs and existence decisions are made and in what a retraction does (Table~\ref{tab:arms}).
Four ablations change one thing each: \arm{AssocOnly} removes the lifecycle (no existence step, hence no retraction, reactivation, dormancy or threshold to select), \arm{NoVersion} makes retraction irreversible, \arm{HeuristicLabel} changes the source of the labels and \arm{HandCost} the costs.
Four rule-based arms re-implement published mechanisms on our interface; \arm{ELU-P}'s initial log-odds, persistence decay and match gain are fitted on training ground truth.
